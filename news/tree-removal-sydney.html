Tree Removal in Sydney: What a Full Job Includes

A full tree removal in Sydney has five parts: a site visit, a council permit and 10/50 check, the removal, green waste removal, and optional stump grinding. A written quote should name all five, because the most common surprise on an invoice is green waste or a stump that the first quote left out.

  • Site visit: the arborist checks height, lean, decay, the root plate and everything within falling distance before choosing a method.
  • Permit check: the tree is checked against your council's tree controls and the NSW 10/50 entitlement.
  • Removal: the tree is limbed first, then the trunk is lowered in sections wherever a straight fell could hit a roof, fence or powerline.
  • Green waste: branches from the tree are mulched on site, and logs are taken away or cut to firewood length, as the quote states.
  • Stump: stump grinding after tree removal is a separate, optional line.

Before work starts, ask for the public liability insurance certificate; many Sydney tree services carry $20 million cover, and an insured arborist will send it on request.

Removing a Tree Close to a House

Removing a tree close to a house costs more, because the arborist has to rope down or lift out every section over the roof instead of dropping it. If a section damages your roof, the company's public liability insurance is what pays, so check the certificate before this kind of job. Some councils make an exception for trees right next to a house: Northern Beaches needs no permit for a tree within 2 m of an approved building.

Falling distance equals tree height: a 20-metre gum standing 15 metres from the house can reach the roof if it fails at the base. Distance alone is still not a reason to remove a healthy tree. Removal makes sense when the trunk near the house is dead, leaning towards it, cracked, hollow, or when roots are lifting footings, paths or pipes.

Dead, Dangerous and Gum Tree Removal

Dead tree removal needs more care: dead wood turns brittle, drops limbs without warning and can snap while someone is working in it. A dead trunk over a house is therefore usually taken down from an elevated platform or with a crane.

Dead trees are not automatically exempt from council rules. The City of Sydney asks for a report from an arborist with at least AQF Level 3, Ku-ring-gai asks you to email photos for written confirmation, and Northern Beaches needs written advice from an AQF Level 5 arborist for an imminently dangerous tree. Large eucalypts are among the most expensive removals in Sydney: a crane job on a gum over 20 m runs $6,500 to $15,000 or more.

Prune or Remove?

Tree removal is the right call only when pruning cannot make the tree safe. An arborist usually decides like this:

What you seeUsual recommendation
Dead tree, or most of the canopy is deadRemove
Split trunk, deep crack or decay through the trunkRemove
New lean with soil lifting on one sideRemove
Weak V-shaped fork on a healthy trunkCable and brace
Long, heavy limbs over the roofReduction pruning to AS 4373

Many councils allow pruning without a permit only for a small share of the canopy (City of Sydney 5%, Northern Beaches 10% in 12 months), so heavy pruning can need the same permit as removal.

The NSW 10/50 entitlement lets owners in designated areas clear trees within 10 metres of a home without approval; check the NSW RFS online tool on the day you clear, because areas change.

Free and Lower-Cost Tree Removal

Free removal is possible only when someone else is responsible:

  • Street and park trees: these belong to the council, and residents need written consent to prune or remove them; report a dangerous one to council.
  • Powerlines: Ausgrid manages trees around its network outside private property, while owners look after their own private lines; within 3 m of a line only accredited workers may cut, so never prune near a line yourself.
  • Firewood: ask whether keeping the logs as firewood lowers the price, since the arborist then has less wood to take away.

Arborists charge the lowest prices for good access and no emergency: storm call-outs cost two to three times more than booked jobs.

Crane Tree Removal

A crane is the arborist's safest option when a large trunk overhangs a house and there is no space to drop wood. The arborist climbs, attaches each section to the crane, makes the cut, and the crane lifts it over the roof to the driveway or street, so heavy wood never falls freely.

  • When the arborist calls a crane: a large or dead trunk over a building, no drop zone, or decay a climber should not stay in for long.
  • Cost: crane jobs on big gums over 20 m run $6,500 to $15,000 or more in Sydney; check that the arborist's quote includes the crane hire and that the crane hire company is insured.
  • Ground protection: mats spread the load on lawns and pavers, and outriggers stay off septic systems and service lines.
  • Street use: a crane on a public road usually needs a council permit, which the quote should mention.

On a big job the crane adds to the arborist's quote but can finish in hours what climbers need days for, which often lowers the total.

Stump Grinding After Tree Removal

Stump grinding after tree removal in Sydney costs $170 to $345 for a small stump under 30 cm, $290 to $575 for 30 to 60 cm and $460 to $1,025 for a stump over 60 cm. Grinding is cheaper than digging out the roots because the machine shreds the stump below ground and the roots decay in the soil.

  • Depth: ask the arborist how deep the stump will be ground: deeper grinding costs more but leaves room to replant or lay turf.
  • Before grinding: lodge a free Before You Dig Australia enquiry and allow up to 2 working days for plans; plans may not show private pipes inside your boundary.
  • Several stumps: grinding several in one visit costs less per stump.

Full root removal is worth paying for only when you plan to build or pour a slab on that exact spot.

How to Get a Written Quote

A good arborist quote is written, itemised and based on a site visit, not a phone guess. Before you book:

  1. Get 2 or 3 written quotes that list the method, green waste and stump separately.
  2. Ask for the public liability insurance certificate; many Sydney tree services carry $20 million cover, so check that the business named on the certificate matches the company quoting you.
  3. Check qualifications: AQF Level 3 for the arborist doing the work, and the level your council asks for in a report (AQF 3 at the City of Sydney, AQF 5 at Northern Beaches for an imminently dangerous tree).
  4. Ask who lodges the council permit and whether 10/50 applies.
  5. Agree what happens to the wood: removal, mulch left on site, or firewood.

Tree Removal Questions

How much does tree removal cost in Sydney?

Tree removal in Sydney costs $290 to $920 for a small tree, $920 to $2,875 for a medium tree and $2,875 to $6,900 for a large tree, with a Sydney average of about $1,840 a job.

Do I need council approval to remove a tree in Sydney?

Council approval to remove a tree in Sydney is usually needed for a tree 5 metres or taller, but each council's DCP sets its own size triggers and exempt species.

Can I remove a tree within 10 metres of my house?

Without council approval, you can remove a tree within 10 metres of your house if your property is in a designated 10/50 area (check the NSW RFS tool on the day you clear) or if the tree falls under one of your council's exemptions, such as size or exempt species.

How much does stump grinding cost in Sydney?

Stump grinding after tree removal in Sydney costs $170 to $345 for a small stump, $290 to $575 for a medium one and $460 to $1,025 for a stump over 60 cm.

How much does an arborist report cost in Sydney?

An arborist report in Sydney costs $345 to $805, typically about $520.

What is the difference between tree lopping and tree removal?

Strictly, lopping cuts branches back to stubs and leaves the tree standing, while tree removal takes the whole tree down; many Sydney businesses use "lopping" for any tree work, so check the quote says removal. The City of Sydney requires a permit to lop a tree that meets its size triggers.
