Tree Removal in London: What a Full Job Includes

A full tree removal in London has five parts: a site visit, a TPO and conservation-area check, the felling, clearing and waste removal, and optional stump grinding. A written quote should name all five, because the most common surprise on an invoice is waste removal or a stump that the first quote left out.

  • Site visit: the tree surgeon checks height, lean, decay, the root plate and everything within falling distance before choosing a method.
  • Permission check: the tree is checked against the council's TPO register and conservation-area maps.
  • Felling: the crown is reduced first, then the trunk is lowered in sections wherever a straight fell could hit a roof, fence or cable.
  • Waste: branches from the tree are chipped on site, and logs are taken away or cut into rounds for firewood, as the quote states.
  • Stump: stump grinding after tree removal is a separate, optional line.

Before work starts, ask for the insurance certificate: public liability insurance of at least £5 million is what the Arboricultural Association recommends, and an insured, qualified tree surgeon will send it without fuss.

Tree jobs with two or more entries in the "higher-cost" column usually need a platform or a crane rather than climbing alone. Many London back gardens are reached only through the house, so branches and brash are carried through by hand, which adds labour time.

Removing a Tree Close to a House

Removing a tree close to a house costs more because every section over the roof has to be roped down or lifted out, not dropped. Tree surgeons call this sectional felling or dismantling, and it raises labour and rigging time.

Falling distance is at least the tree's height: a 20-metre conifer standing 15 metres from the house can reach the roof if it fails at the base. Distance alone is still not a reason to remove a healthy tree. Removal makes sense when the trunk near the house is dead, leaning towards it, cracked, hollow through a large part of the trunk, or when roots are lifting foundations, paths or drains.

You may cut a neighbour's branches that overhang your garden back to the boundary, but not beyond it, and you must offer the cuttings back to the tree's owner. Check the tree for a TPO first.

Dead Tree and Conifer Removal

Dead tree removal needs more care: dead wood turns brittle, drops limbs without warning and can snap while someone is working in it. A dead trunk over a house is therefore usually taken down from a platform or with a crane, not by climbing.

Conifers such as leylandii and pines are easy to cut, but a mature conifer is tall and top-heavy, so falling distance sets the price. A healthy conifer with open ground around it can stay; one that is dead, leaning or cracked within reach of the house should come down. A protected dead conifer still needs 5 working days' written notice to the council.

Prune, Brace or Remove?

Tree removal is the right call only when neither pruning nor bracing can make the tree safe. A tree surgeon usually decides like this:

What you seeUsual recommendation
Dead tree, or most of the crown is deadRemove
Split trunk, deep crack or decay through the trunkRemove
New lean with soil lifting on one sideRemove
Weak V-shaped fork on a healthy trunkCrown bracing
Long, heavy limbs over the roofCrown reduction

Pruning should not remove too much of the live crown in one visit; a tree that needs heavier cutting to be safe is usually a removal candidate.

Do You Need Permission to Remove a Tree in London?

Permission to remove a tree in London depends on two checks with your borough council: whether the tree has a Tree Preservation Order, and whether it stands in a conservation area. Councils keep a TPO register you can inspect, and you can ask the planning department directly.

SituationWhat you must do
Tree Preservation OrderApply to the council on the standard form or the Planning Portal, with a plan, the work and your reasons
Conservation area, stem over 75 mm at 1.5 mGive 6 weeks' notice (Section 211 notice)
Dead protected treeGive 5 working days' written notice
Dangerous protected treeGive notice as soon as practicable
Garden, no TPO or conservation areaNo consent or felling licence needed

Cutting down a protected tree without consent carries an unlimited fine. The GOV.UK guidance on Tree Preservation Orders sets out each procedure.

Wildlife law applies to tree work even when no permission is needed: it is an offence to damage an active bird's nest, and bat roosts are protected all year. The main nesting season is commonly cited as March to August, so book non-urgent felling for autumn or winter.

Free and Lower-Cost Tree Removal

Free tree removal is possible only when someone else is responsible for the tree, and overhead lines are a job for the network operator first:

  • Street and park trees: these belong to the borough council; report a dangerous one to the council rather than hiring a contractor.
  • Power lines: never cut near an overhead line yourself; contact UK Power Networks (105) first, which can inspect or arrange a shutdown so your contractor can work; allow up to 15 working days for a visit.

Autumn and winter suit non-urgent felling: the nesting season is over and bare crowns are lighter to handle.

Crane Tree Removal

Crane tree removal is the safest option when a large trunk overhangs a house and there is no space to drop wood. The climber attaches each section to the crane, makes the cut, and the crane lifts it over the roof to the road, so heavy wood never falls freely.

  • When a crane is used: large or dead trees over a building, no drop zone, or decay a climber should not stay in for long.
  • Cost: crane hire adds £1,000 to £2,500 or more to the price, and the crane firm carries its own insurance for the lift.
  • Ground protection: mats spread the load on lawns and paving, and outriggers stay clear of drains and service runs.
  • Road use: a crane on a London street may need a council licence or parking suspension, which the quote should mention.

On large trees a crane costs extra but can finish in hours what climbers need days for, which can offset part of the hire cost.

Stump Grinding After Tree Removal

Stump grinding costs £100 to £300 per stump in London; full root removal costs more and is rarely needed. Grinding is cheaper because the machine shreds the stump below ground and the roots decay in the soil.

  • Depth: deep enough for turf, deeper where you plan to replant.
  • Before grinding: run a free LinesearchbeforeUdig (LSBUD) search so the cable and pipe owners can send plans.
  • Several stumps: grinding several in one visit costs less per stump.

Full root removal is worth paying for only when you plan to build or lay a base on that exact spot.

How to Get a Written Quote

A good quote for felling trees is written, itemised and based on a site visit, not a phone guess. Before you book:

  1. Get two or three written quotes that list the method, waste and stump separately.
  2. Ask for proof of public liability insurance of at least £5 million.
  3. Check qualifications: ARB Approved Contractor status in the Arboricultural Association directory, and NPTC certificates CS38 and CS39 for climbing work.
  4. Ask who handles the TPO or Section 211 notice.
  5. Agree what happens to the wood: removal, or rounds left for firewood.

Work should follow BS 3998:2010, the British Standard for tree work, and the insurance certificate should name the company on the quote; do not accept one issued in another name.

Tree Removal Questions

How much does it cost to remove a tree in the UK?

The average cost to remove a tree in the UK is £150 to £350 for a tree under 25 ft and £400 to £700 for 25 to 50 ft; London prices are 20% to 40% higher, with a small tree from £250.

How much does it cost to remove a large tree in London?

Removing a large tree in London costs £900 to £1,900 or more by one London guide, and £1,200 to £3,500 or more for trees over 30 ft by another; crane hire adds £1,000 to £2,500 or more.

How much does it cost to remove a tree stump in the UK?

Removing a tree stump by grinding costs £60 to £400 per stump in the UK and £100 to £300 in London, depending on diameter and access.

Do I need permission to cut down a tree in my garden?

You need council consent to cut down a tree in your garden if it has a Tree Preservation Order, and 6 weeks' notice if it stands in a conservation area; otherwise garden trees need no felling licence.

Can I cut down a tree during nesting season?

Tree felling during nesting season is not banned, but damaging or destroying a nest in use is an offence, so have active nests checked first; March to August is commonly cited as the main season.

What qualifications should a tree surgeon have?

A tree surgeon should hold City & Guilds NPTC certificates such as CS30 and CS31 (chainsaw), CS38 (climbing and aerial rescue) and CS39 (chainsaw from rope and harness), be insured for public liability, and ideally be an ARB Approved Contractor.
