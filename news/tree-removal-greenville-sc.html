Tree Removal in Greenville, SC: What a Full Job Includes

A full tree removal in Greenville, SC has five parts: an on-site assessment, a permit check, the takedown, cleanup and haul-away, and optional stump grinding. A written quote should name all five, because the most common surprise on a final bill is a cleanup or stump line that the first quote left out.

  • Assessment: the crew lead checks the tree's height, lean, decay, the root plate and everything within falling distance before choosing a method.
  • Permit check: tree rules for addresses inside City of Greenville limits are checked, and so are county and HOA rules.
  • Takedown: limbs come off the tree first, then the trunk is lowered in sections wherever a straight fell could hit a roof, fence or line.
  • Cleanup: brush from the tree is chipped on site, and logs are hauled away or cut to firewood length and stacked, as the estimate states.
  • Stump: stump grinding after tree removal is a separate, optional line so you can decide after seeing the price.

Before any tree removal starts, ask the company for a certificate of insurance covering liability and workers' compensation. On our jobs, the remove-or-keep call on a large tree is made by our partner arborist James Martin, ISA Certified Arborist SO-356834A.

Removing a Tree Close to a House

Removing a tree close to a house typically costs $500 to $3,000 or more, because every piece over the roof has to be roped down or lifted out instead of dropped. Distance guides help decide whether a healthy tree is too close:

Tree size at maturityExampleDistance from the foundation
Small, under 25 ftDogwoodAt least 10 ft
Medium, 25 to 50 ftSmall maple15 to 20 ft
Large, over 50 ftOak, tall pine20 to 30 ft or more

Falling distance equals tree height: a 70-foot pine standing 50 feet from the house can reach the roof if it fails at the base. Distance alone is still not a reason to remove a healthy tree. Removal makes sense when the tree near the house is dead, leaning toward it, cracked, hollow through a third of the trunk, or when roots are lifting the foundation, walkways or sewer lines. Before removal day, move cars, grills and patio furniture at least one tree-height away and unlock gates for the crew.

Dead Tree and Pine Tree Removal

Dead tree removal costs about the same as a living tree of the same size, but it needs more caution: dead wood turns brittle, drops limbs without warning and can collapse while someone is cutting. A dead trunk over a house usually comes down from a bucket truck or by crane.

Pine tree removal cost in the Upstate follows local job sizes: one Greenville-area company lists $500 to $700 for small jobs, $700 to $1,800 for medium and $1,800 to $4,500 for large ones. Pine trees are softwood and cut quickly, but a mature pine is tall and top-heavy, so the falling distance decides the price.

Are pine trees hard to remove? Pine trees are easier to cut than dense oaks, yet a tall pine near a house is hard to remove safely because its weight sits high in the crown and its shallow roots can let go in wet soil. A healthy pine with open ground around it can stay; a pine that is dead, leaning, cracked or within reach of the house should come down.

Prune, Cable or Remove?

Removal is the right call only when pruning or cabling cannot make the tree safe. This is how an arborist usually decides:

What you seeUsual recommendation
Dead tree, or most of the crown is deadRemove
Split trunk, deep crack or decay through the trunkRemove
New lean with soil lifting on one sideRemove
Weak V-shaped fork on a healthy trunkCable and brace
Long, heavy limbs over the roofPrune to reduce weight

Pruning should take no more than 25% of the live crown in one visit; a tree that needs more than that to be safe is usually a removal candidate.

Do You Need a Permit to Remove a Tree in Greenville, SC?

City of Greenville tree removal permit requirements apply to properties inside city limits, under the city's tree preservation ordinance. Whether you need a permit to remove a tree in Greenville, SC therefore depends on where the address is: the ordinance can require city review for large or protected trees and for any land disturbance; the Greenville Cares Information Center at 864-232-2273 confirms whether a specific tree needs a permit.

  • Inside city limits: the city's tree rules apply; permit requests go through its online forms, and its map shows protected trees.
  • Unincorporated Greenville County: a single-family yard usually faces fewer rules.
  • Other towns: Simpsonville, Greer and Mauldin set their own rules.
  • HOA: many neighborhoods require written approval even where the city does not.
  • Dead or dangerous trees: these are usually handled faster, but take dated photos before cutting in case a question comes up later.

Greenville County's tree ordinance requirements (Tree Ordinance No. 4173) mainly regulate trees on new developments, commercial sites and multi-unit projects rather than single-family yards.

Many Greenville mailing addresses are outside the city limits, so confirm the jurisdiction first; the estimate visit includes this check.

Free and Lower-Cost Tree Removal Options

Free tree removal is possible only when someone else is responsible for the tree:

  • Power lines: Duke Energy, the utility for most of Greenville County, trims or removes trees that threaten its distribution lines at no cost to the homeowner; trees near the service line to your house are usually your responsibility, though the utility can disconnect it for safe work.
  • Public right-of-way: limbs or trunks blocking a city street or sidewalk are reported to Greenville Cares, and the city clears them.
  • Trading the wood for the work: rarely works for pines, because pine has low timber value.

Free removal programs for seniors are rare, so ask each company about discounts directly. The cheapest season for tree removal is late fall through winter, November to February, when storm work slows down and leafless crowns are lighter to handle.

Crane Tree Removal for Large Trees

Crane tree removal is the safest method when a large trunk leans over a house and there is no open ground to drop wood. A climber goes up, attaches a rigging sling to each section, makes the cut, and the crane lifts the piece over the roof to the driveway or street, so heavy wood never free-falls.

  • When a crane is used: a large or dead trunk over a structure, no drop zone, or a climber who should not stay long in decayed wood.
  • Crane size: most residential jobs use a 30- to 40-ton boom truck or hydraulic crane; tall hardwoods may need a larger class.
  • Ground protection: mats spread the load across lawns and driveways, and the operator keeps outriggers off septic fields and drain lines.
  • Street use: a crane parked on a public street may need city or HOA notice, which the estimate should mention.

Crane tree removal in a tight Greenville yard typically runs $1,500 to $4,500 or more; national guides list $2,000 to $15,000 or more for very large or storm-damaged lifts. The crane and operator alone often cost $500 to $1,500 or more per day, yet a crane can finish in hours what a climbing crew needs days for, which often lowers the total on big trees. Crane work in tree care follows the ANSI Z133 safety standard.

Stump Removal Cost: Grinding vs Digging Out

Stump removal cost after tree removal is $150 to $450 for grinding a typical single stump, while digging out the whole root ball runs $300 to $1,000 or more. Stump grinding prices in Greenville, SC follow the same range. Grinding is cheaper because the machine shreds the stump below the soil and leaves the roots to decay.

  • By size: many tree crews charge $2 to $5 per inch of stump diameter, with a $100 to $150 minimum.
  • Extra stumps: often $30 to $60 each when ground in the same visit.
  • Depth: about 6 inches below grade for lawn, 8 to 12 inches where you plan to plant.
  • Before grinding: SC 811 must mark buried utility lines, which takes 3 full business days after the request.

Full root removal is worth paying for only when you plan to build or pour concrete on that exact spot.

How to Get a Written Estimate

A good tree removal estimate is written, itemized and based on an on-site look, not a phone guess. Before you sign with any company:

  1. Get two or three written quotes that list the method, cleanup and stump as separate lines.
  2. Ask for a certificate of insurance sent by the insurer, covering general liability and workers' compensation; a company that is properly insured sends it without delay.
  3. Verify the arborist on the ISA credential search.
  4. Confirm the permit question for your address and who files any paperwork.
  5. Agree on the wood: hauled away, or cut to length and stacked.

Tree Removal Questions

How much does it cost to cut down a 30-foot tree?

Cutting down a 30-foot tree usually costs $150 to $500 with easy access, and taking down a 30-foot tree near a house or with rigging costs up to about $800. Those are national averages; grinding the stump adds $150 to $450.

What is the average cost to cut a tree down?

The average cost to cut a tree down is about $750 nationally, with most Greenville-area jobs between $250 and $2,500 before stump grinding.

What's the average cost to cut down a 40-foot pine tree?

The average cost to cut down a 40-foot pine tree is $435 to $1,200 nationally, and 25% to 50% more when the pine stands close to a house, fence or power line. Greenville-area companies list about $700 to $1,800 for a job that size.

What is the cheapest time of year for tree removal?

The cheapest time of year for tree removal is typically late fall through winter, November through February, when tree removal companies are less busy after storm season and may offer off-peak rates; leafless crowns are lighter and faster to cut and clean up; and firm winter ground keeps equipment from rutting the lawn.

How do you get a tree taken down for free?

A tree, even a large one, is taken down for free only when someone else, such as the utility or the city, is responsible for it; otherwise professional removal of a big tree in Greenville costs $1,800 to $4,500 or more. Duke Energy clears trees that threaten its distribution lines at no charge, and the city clears trees blocking a public street or sidewalk. Trading the wood rarely works for pines, which have low timber value.

Does it cost more to remove a dead tree?

A dead tree costs about the same to remove as a living one of the same size, and more when brittle wood over a house means the crew must work from a bucket truck or crane.

Is stump grinding included in the tree removal price?

Stump grinding is usually a separate line in a tree removal quote, typically $150 to $450 per stump, so check every quote for it.

What size crane is used for tree removal?

Tree removal cranes for most homes are 30- to 40-ton boom trucks or hydraulic cranes; very tall hardwoods may need 50 tons or more.
